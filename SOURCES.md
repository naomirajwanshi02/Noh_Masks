# Sources

Every fact in the app (`js/data.js`), with where it comes from. These were checked
on 26 and 28 Oct 2026.

## About Noh and Kyogen

- "Noh has been performed in Japan since the 1300s." UNESCO says Nōgaku had its
  heyday in the 14th and 15th centuries, when Kan'ami and Zeami shaped the form.
  - [UNESCO: Nôgaku theatre](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/nogaku-theatre-00012)
- Masks are tilted up to look happy (*terasu*, "to shine") and down to look sad
  (*kumorasu*, "to cloud").
  - [the-noh.com glossary: Terasu](https://db2.the-noh.com/edic/2008/11/terasu.html)
  - [the-noh.com glossary: Kumorasu](https://db2.the-noh.com/edic/2008/11/kumorasu.html)

## The masks

| Mask | What the app says | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| **Kojo** 小尉 | Worn by the old man in the first half of *Takasago*. He sweeps under a pine and turns out to be a pine spirit. His hair and beard are implanted horsehair. | **Hannya** 般若 | A woman turned demon through jealousy. She looks angry from the front and seems to cry when tilted down. A **red** Hannya is used for the girl's spirit in *Dōjōji*. | [Wikipedia: Hannya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannya) · [yokai.jp: Hannya](https://yokai.jp/en/yokai/hannya) |

| **Saru** 猿 | The monkey of the Kyogen play *Utsubozaru*. Children of Kyogen families usually debut at about age 4 as the little monkey. | [Kids Web Japan: Kyogen](https://web-japan.org/kidsweb/cool/13-12-16/002.html) |
| **Kitsune** 狐 | In the Kyogen play *Tsurigitsune* an old fox disguises himself as the priest Hakuzōsu. ## Noh or Kyogen?

All eight are real mask types from the two theatres of Nōgaku (UNESCO lists Noh and
Kyogen together as [Nôgaku theatre](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/nogaku-theatre-00012)).

## Honest notes on the designs

- Each mask's look follows the reference paintings supplied for this project.
- **Hannya** is red, as used in *Dōjōji* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannya)).
- **Kitsune** is a Kyogen mask type, but this design follows the reference painting, a
  weathered cream fox with red markings in the style of festival fox masks.
  Museum Kyogen fox masks (for *Tsurigitsune*) look different: carved wood in natural
  tones, some with bared fangs or folded ears, so this one is not a faithful Kyogen mask.
- The masks are made in code. They are close interpretations, not copies of
  particular museum pieces.
